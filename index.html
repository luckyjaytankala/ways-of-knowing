REAL-WORLD CASE / H. PYLORISOURCE [1]

The stomach
plot twist.

In the early 1980s, stress and lifestyle dominated explanations of peptic ulcers. Barry Marshall and Robin Warren challenged that view by investigating Helicobacter pylori.

Their work connected a bacterium to gastritis and peptic ulcer disease. They received the 2005 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

The twist was a bacterium. The lesson is about evidence.

OPEN THE EVIDENCE FILE0 / 3 OPENED

Reveal each piece. Decide how much it justifies.

Observation

Warren observed bacteria near inflamed stomach tissue.

A reason to investigate. Association alone leaves alternative explanations open.

Investigation

Researchers cultured the bacterium, making it available for further study.

Reason connects the hypothesis to predictions that researchers can investigate.

Converging evidence

Treatment studies linked eliminating the infection with lasting recovery in H. pylori-related ulcer disease.

Different kinds of evidence strengthen the causal explanation. This does not mean every ulcer has the same cause.

Case reviewed: scientific reasoning becomes stronger when observations and tests support the same explanation.

FIRST-ORDER QUESTION

What role does H. pylori play in peptic ulcer disease?

SECOND-ORDER KNOWLEDGE QUESTION

When does evidence justify replacing an accepted scientific explanation?

Why reason helps

It links a hypothesis to testable consequences and helps compare explanations.

Where it meets a limit

A logical explanation still needs empirical support. A plausible story can fit a pattern without establishing its cause.

A question to take further +

If an expert rejects a new hypothesis, when is that reasonable caution and when is it resistance to change? Compare the evidence available at the time, rather than judging only with hindsight.

REAL-WORLD CASE / FOUNTAIN, 1917SOURCES [2] [4]

Art. Plumbing.
Both?

In 1917, a urinal titled Fountain and signed “R. Mutt” was submitted to an exhibition. The committee rejected it. Associated with Marcel Duchamp, it became a major example of the readymade.

A bathroom fixture started a very long discussion.

Choose a criterion. Watch the argument change.

These are simplified interpretive positions, not quotations or a vote on the correct definition of art.

FIRST-ORDER QUESTION

How did presenting an ordinary object as Fountain challenge conventions of art?

SECOND-ORDER KNOWLEDGE QUESTION

To what extent can reason justify a judgment about what counts as art?

Why reason helps

It makes the criteria behind a judgment visible, so people can debate them.

Where it meets a limit

A valid argument cannot settle a disagreement if people reject its definition of art. Interpretation also draws on experience and emotion.

A question to take further +

If artistic value depends on context, could a museum make any object valuable? Explain a criterion, then test it against a counterexample. “It’s all subjective” still leaves the reasoning unfinished.

ALTERNATE THEME / KNOWLEDGE & TECHNOLOGYSOURCE [3]

It sounds certain.
Should you be?

A polished AI answer can contain false information. NIST describes this risk as confabulation: confidently presented content that is erroneous or false.

Reason helps us separate a claim from its delivery. A citation needs checking, and a conclusion needs to follow from what the source actually says.

An answer can have excellent grammar and terrible evidence.

FICTIONAL AI ANSWER / CREATED FOR THIS ACTIVITY
“Students who use StudySprint score 40% higher. Therefore, StudySprint causes better grades.”

StudySprint and this statistic are invented. There is no real study behind this example.

What should you check first?

QUESTION ABOUT THE EXAMPLE

What evidence would be needed to test StudySprint’s supposed effect?

KNOWLEDGE QUESTION

How should knowers justify trust in an explanation generated by a system they cannot fully inspect?

A useful tool

AI can suggest explanations and counterarguments that a knower can investigate.

A knower’s responsibility

Fluent output does not establish factual accuracy. Verify the original evidence and consider alternative causes before accepting a conclusion.

A question to take further +

We already depend on experts whose full reasoning we cannot personally inspect. What makes trust in an AI explanation similar or different? Consider expertise, transparency, independent checking, and accountability.
